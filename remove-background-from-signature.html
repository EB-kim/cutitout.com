How to remove the background from a signature for free

A transparent-PNG signature you can drop onto contracts, PDFs, and email — made entirely in your browser.

You signed a page, scanned or photographed it, and now you want just the ink — no white paper, no gray shadow — so you can place it on a document. Here's the fastest way to get a clean transparent PNG signature without uploading your signature to a stranger's server, and without paying or signing up.

Why not just upload it somewhere?

A signature is sensitive. Uploading it to an unknown cloud service to "remove the background" means handing over an image that can be reused. The tool on this site does all the work locally in your browser — your signature never leaves your device.

Step by step

  1. Get a good source image. Sign in dark ink on clean white paper. Photograph or scan it straight-on with even lighting to avoid shadows.
  2. Open the tool and drop your image in.
  3. Magic Wand the white. Set tolerance around 30–50 and click the white area around your signature. Connected white pixels vanish, leaving the ink.
  4. Clean the fringes. Switch to the Eraser to remove leftover gray edges or stray marks. Use Restore if you erase part of a letter.
  5. Export PNG. You get a transparent signature ready to place in Word, Google Docs, a PDF signer, or an email footer.

Tips for a crisp result

  • Higher-contrast ink (black gel pen) cuts out far more cleanly than faint pencil.
  • If the wand leaves a halo, lower the tolerance and click again, then finish with the eraser.
  • Sign near the top-left of the page and photograph tight to the signature — the exported PNG keeps the same dimensions as your source image, so a tightly-framed photo means less empty space.

FAQ

Will my signature be uploaded?

No. It is processed only in your browser and is discarded when you close the tab.

What if the paper has a shadow?

Increase the wand tolerance a little, then use the eraser to remove the darker shadow edge the wand missed.

Can I put it on a white background instead?

Yes — use "Export on White" for a flattened version.
