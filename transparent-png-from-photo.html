How to make a transparent PNG from a photo for free

Cut a product or object out of its background and export a transparent PNG — for listings, slide decks, and thumbnails — right in your browser.

Open the free cutout tool →

Marketplace listings, presentations, and thumbnails look far more professional when the product floats on its own instead of sitting in a cluttered photo. That means a transparent PNG — the object with the background removed. Here's how to make one for free, with no upload and no signup.

Why do it in your browser?

Many "free" background removers upload your photo to their servers and add a watermark or a paywall at export. The tool on this site runs entirely on your device — your photo never leaves your browser, and there's no watermark.

Step by step

  1. Shoot or pick a clean photo. An object on a plain, evenly-lit background (a wall, a sheet of paper) is easiest to cut out.
  2. Open the tool and drop the photo in.
  3. Magic Wand the background. Set tolerance around 25–45 and click the background. Adjust and click again until the background is gone.
  4. Refine the edges. Use the Eraser for stray background near the object, and Restore if you remove part of the product.
  5. Export. Save a transparent PNG, or use "Export on White" if the marketplace requires a clean white background.

Tips for a clean cutout

  • Even lighting with no harsh shadows makes the background one consistent color the wand can grab in a click or two.
  • More contrast between the object and the background = a cleaner edge.
  • For fuzzy edges (hair, fur, plants), expect to finish by hand with the eraser.
  • Many marketplaces (eBay, Etsy, Amazon) prefer a pure white background — "Export on White" gives you that from the same cutout.

FAQ

Is my photo uploaded?

No. It's processed only in your browser and discarded when you close the tab.

Is there a watermark?

No. The exported PNG is clean.

What if the background is busy?

The magic wand works best on plain backgrounds. On a busy one, expect more manual erasing — or reshoot against a plain wall or paper.
