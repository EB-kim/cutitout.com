How to make a logo transparent for free

Get a transparent-background logo PNG for your website, letterhead, invoices, and merch — made entirely in your browser.

Open the free cutout tool →

A logo saved as a JPG almost always comes with a white box around it. Drop that on a colored website header or a photo and the ugly rectangle shows. What you want is a transparent PNG — the mark with nothing behind it — so it sits cleanly on any background. Here's how to make one for free, without uploading your brand assets to a random server or paying for a design app.

Why not just upload it to an online tool?

Your logo is a brand asset. Uploading it to an unknown service means handing over a file that can be cached, reused, or scraped. The tool on this site processes the image locally in your browser — your logo never leaves your device.

Step by step

  1. Start with the cleanest source you have. A logo on a solid white (or single flat color) background cuts out best. A high-resolution PNG or JPG is ideal.
  2. Open the tool and drop your logo in.
  3. Magic Wand the background. Set tolerance around 20–40 and click the background area. Connected background pixels disappear, leaving the mark. If your logo has enclosed areas (like the hole in an "O"), click those too.
  4. Tidy the edges. Switch to the Eraser to remove any leftover fringe. Use Restore if you clip part of the logo.
  5. Export PNG. You get a transparent logo ready for your site, email signature, slide deck, or print file.

Tips for a crisp logo cutout

  • Solid-color backgrounds cut out far more cleanly than gradients or textures.
  • If a faint halo remains, lower the tolerance and click again, then finish with the eraser.
  • Anti-aliased (soft) edges may leave a thin outline — zoom in and erase gently for a clean silhouette.
  • Keep an untouched master copy of your logo; export the transparent version as a new file.

FAQ

Will my logo be uploaded anywhere?

No. It is processed only in your browser and discarded when you close the tab.

My logo has a gradient background — will this work?

Gradients are harder for the wand. Raise the tolerance and clean up with the eraser, or start from a version on a flat background if you have one.

Can I keep a white background instead?

Yes — use "Export on White" for a flattened version.
